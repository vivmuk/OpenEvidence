Model Stack

Which underlying model actually powers each clinical AI tool — and how confident we are, with a dated clickable source for every claim.

Why this is hard to state

Vendors rarely publish which model is under the hood. They publish which capability they have. A model named in a blog post 8 months ago may have been replaced twice since. So every claim here carries the date it was true, the source it came from, and how it was verified — and where a tool simply does not disclose its model, that is what it says.

Conflict rule

Where sources disagree, the most recent dated statement wins — typically a vendor release note or model card, since vendors update their own claims first. The losing claim is kept and shown, not deleted, with the resolution explained. Where nothing is dated, the oldest verifiable statement wins over a newer undated one.

Confidence vocabulary

Disclosed the vendor states the model. Reported a credible third party states it. Inferred deduced from other evidence — the reasoning is shown. Undisclosed no public statement; an honest answer, not a gap.

What this shows

Clinician-facing AI has converged on a small number of frontier model families — GPT, Claude, Gemini — used either directly or fine-tuned into a vertical clinical model. The interesting differences are not the base model but what sits on top: the retrieval corpus, whether answers are grounded with citations, whether the clinician can pick the model, and how quickly the vendor ships a new one.

The disclosure gap

Ambient documentation vendors and consumer health apps disclose far less than foundation-model labs and EHR vendors. That asymmetry is itself the finding: for several tools below, the model is genuinely not public, and no amount of inference from benchmark results can substitute for a vendor statement.

Caveats

Model names in this section are what the cited source said on the cited date — not a guarantee of what ships today, and enterprise contracts can pin a version for months. Verify against the primary source before relying on any claim. Benchmarks measure a configuration, not a product.
